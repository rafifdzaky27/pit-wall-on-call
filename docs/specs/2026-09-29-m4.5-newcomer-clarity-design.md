# M4.5: clear for newcomers (design)

## Why

On 2026-09-29, the first outside usability test was run with someone who does not work in DevOps. It found:
- They could not tell what the game is for or what the goal is ("how to fix it?").
- They did not know which service to look at, so they clicked through services one by one.
- The first-shift notices did not say what to do next.
- Chat lines such as "nightly batch jobs" confused them about who is responsible.
- Monitoring seemed to show only checkout.
- They asked for a legend "like a map": what critical and warn mean, and short explanations of DevOps terms.

The game stays built for engineers first, but a curious newcomer must be able to follow it (the M2.5 decision Q4). Nothing here may give away an incident's cause: guidance points at *where to look*, never at *what is wrong* (the M4 fairness rules still hold).

## N1. The objective

Every shift states its goal in plain words, from the moment the page arrives:
- **What is broken for customers:** the page title and the Browser symptom.
- **What winning means:** find the cause and fix it before the error budget runs out; a fix that only hides the symptom does not count.
- **Where the clock is:** time and budget left.

It appears in three places: the ack notice ("You're on it"), a compact "Your job" strip at the top of Monitoring, and the Incident app. The objective is generic and built from scenario data (page title, symptom, services), never from the cause.

## N2. The legend and terms in place

- **Monitoring's service map legend:** OK, Warn, Critical and what each means for customers, plus "Click a service to see its metrics and checks". Every node reads as clickable (cursor, hover, focus ring, selected state), and the map says how many services there are, so it never looks like checkout is the only one.
- **Severity** (SEV1–3) and alert levels are explained through `Term` tooltips.
- **The glossary** grows to cover the words the eight new incidents use: replication slot, WAL, replica lag, dead-letter queue, consumer lag, offset, circuit breaker, retries and backoff, cache hit ratio, request coalescing, TLS certificate, config rollback, WAF rule, disk volume, log level, deploy vs config change.

  The terms are wrapped in `Term` where the tools show them: Logs, Deploys, DB console, Monitoring metric names and chat headers. Definitions are plain language and never name an incident's cause ("A dead-letter queue is where messages that cannot be processed are set aside", not "move the bad message there").

## N3. Next-step guidance for first shifts

A light guide, on by default for players who have not resolved a shift yet, and switchable in Settings → Gameplay ("Show next-step hints"). It is separate from the training coach and from the duck.
- It watches progress milestones: acknowledged; opened Monitoring; selected the critical service; ran a check or saw logs; looked at what changed (Deploys or #deploys); took a fix action.
- After a quiet spell (about 45 s with no new milestone), it shows one notice pointing at the next *place*.

  Example: "The red service on the map is where customers' errors come from. Open Monitoring and click it". Later: "Something changed? Deploys and the #deploys channel show what shipped today". Later: "Logs tell you what the failing service says about itself".
- Hints are built from generic milestones and scenario data (which service is critical right now), and never name the fix, the cause or a spoiler word (N4 of the M4 spec).
- At most one hint per milestone. None after the first resolved shift, unless re-enabled.
- Chat messages from teammates keep their voice. The guide can say "Read #deploys: teammates post what they changed", so "nightly batch jobs" reads as a clue to weigh, not an instruction.

## Out of scope

Rewriting incidents, new tools, and localisation (the game stays English).

## Tests

- **Objective:** unit tests that it renders from scenario data and contains no spoiler word, over every variant.
- **Guide:** timing, the milestone state machine, the off switch, a per-variant spoiler test, and none after the first resolved shift.
- **Legend:** renders the labels, and the map nodes are buttons with visible names.
- **Glossary:** every term used through `Term` exists.
- **e2e:** a first-time practice shift shows the objective and a hint after a quiet spell.
