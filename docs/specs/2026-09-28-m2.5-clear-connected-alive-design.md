# M2.5: Clear, connected, alive — design spec

- **Status:** written 2026-09-28 from Rafif's playtest and Claude's scripted walkthrough (research synthesis in the session). Decisions Q1–Q5 were answered by Rafif. Implemented straight away, in three PRs under one milestone.
- **Parent specs:** `2026-09-27-pit-wall-on-call-design.md`, `2026-09-28-cold-open-design.md`, `2026-09-28-pitos-desktop-design.md`, `2026-09-28-runs-api-leaderboard-design.md`.
- **Why:** the game has a lot to look at and too little to do. Players cannot tell whether they are right or done, nothing tells them what to do next, the tools are not connected, the transitions jump, and the café is flat and empty.

## 3. Incident status (PR A)

The engine snapshot gains a read-only `status`, derived each tick; scoring does not change.

| Status | When | Shown as |
|---|---|---|
| `paging` | Paged, not acknowledged | "Paged · acknowledge" (crit) |
| `investigating` | Acknowledged, no mitigation, root cause active | "Investigating" (warn) |
| `mitigated` | The scenario's `mitigated(state)` holds: symptoms down, cause still active | "Mitigated · cause still active" (warn) |
| `holding` | The resolve condition holds and the 10 s timer runs | "Fix holding · 7 s" (info) |
| `resolved` / `dnf` | Run over | "Resolved" (ok) / "Out of time" (crit) |

- **Where:** a status chip in the PitOS top bar (replacing "On call · Primary" during a shift), in the Dashboards header, and in the Incident app. The chip is a button that opens the Incident app.
- **Mitigated needs an explanation, not a spoiler.** Entering `mitigated` pushes a notification: "Symptoms are down, but the incident is still open. Is the cause fixed, or only its effect?" It also starts the teammate prompts in §7.
- **Postmortem:** mitigations that only hid the symptom get a line: "Fail over to replica reset the pool, so the errors stopped. The leak in v142 kept running."

## 4. Help and the incident checklist (PR A)

- **"?" button** in the top bar tray (and the `?` key). It opens **Help**, a PitOS app with four pages:
  1. **How to play:** the loop in six short lines, with the keyboard.
  2. **Checklist:** the incident checklist below.
  3. **Glossary:** plain-language definitions (error budget, 5xx, 502, p99, connection pool, idle in transaction, rollback, failover, max_connections, SEV2, ack, on-call, secondary, mitigation versus fix, status page).
  4. **Tools:** what each app is for.
- **The checklist**, modelled on real incident response (Google SRE incident command, PagerDuty's response guide). It ticks itself from the timeline:
  1. Acknowledge the page.
  2. Check the impact: what customers see (Browser, support, the error rate).
  3. Form a hypothesis: investigate at least two services.
  4. Stop the bleeding: mitigate or fix.
  5. Verify: the fix holds for 10 s, with no alerts.
  6. Communicate: post a status update.
  7. Close: read the postmortem.

  The checklist never names the answer.
- **Glossary terms in context:** the first mention of a glossary term in Dashboards, Logs and Deploys has a dotted underline and a tooltip (hover or focus).

## 5. Training shift (PR A)

- **New scenario `training-config-push`, "Training: The Bad Config".** Three services (edge → api → redis). A config push sets the api's Redis timeout to 5 ms, the api returns 500 on cache misses, and rolling the config back fixes it. Time limit 4 minutes. It has golden tests like every scenario.
- **Entry:** the shift card offers "Start shift" and "Training shift (about 3 min)". The first visit highlights Training ("New here? Start with the training shift"). Settings → Account remembers whether it was completed.
- **Coach:** the secondary on-call coaches in a DM, one step at a time, and the step shows in a small coach card with a **Show me** button that outlines the control to use. Steps: acknowledge, open Dashboards, pick the red service, read its logs, check recent deploys, roll back, watch the fix hold, post a status update, read the results.
- **Scoring:** never posted. The results card says "Training complete" and offers the real shift.

## 6. Results card (PR A)

- **Automatic**, as in Wordle: after the cold close caption (§11), the results card opens over the café by itself.
- **Contents:**
  - Resolved in mm:ss, or Out of time.
  - Budget burned, mitigated and confirmed times, root cause found, clues found.
  - The leaderboard: posting state, rank ("#12 of 340"), the top 5 plus your own row. The handle field sits here when there is no handle yet.
  - **Share:** copies the spoiler-free text of parent spec §7 (squares per action verdict).
  - Buttons: **New shift** (primary), Read the postmortem, Full leaderboard.
- **Postmortem:** keeps the full debrief and the Leaderboard panel.

## 7. Chat that works (PR B)

- **Composer:** every channel and DM has a composer with suggested replies (chips) for the current moment, and accepts slash commands:

  | Command | Maps to |
  |---|---|
  | `/ask @name <topic>` | a teammate question |
  | `/status <text>` | Post status update, with the player's own text |
  | `/page @secondary` | Ask secondary on-call |
  | `/ic` | take incident command |
  | `/help` | help |

  Free text posts as a message; teammates only react to things they can answer ("I'm not sure what you mean. Try /help.").
- **Asking is asynchronous:** a teammate question is an engine action that does not block the player. The reply lands after its delay, with a typing indicator. Answers are deterministic scenario content, and some mislead. For example, infra says the DB is noisy because of batch jobs.
- **Teammates notice.** Scenario messages can trigger on status and time:
  - in `mitigated` for 20 s, the secondary writes: "5xx are gone after the failover, but checkout's pool is climbing again (71/100). Is the cause actually fixed?"
  - support reports customer impact while errors burn.
- **Links:** deploy cards, log lines and teammate answers link to the tool that shows more ("Open in Deploys").

## 8. Tools as separate apps (PR B)

The single Monitoring window becomes the tools an SRE really switches between. Every action lives in the tool where it would be done:

| App | Like | Holds |
|---|---|---|
| **Dashboards** | Grafana | the service map, metric panels per service, alerts. Free to look at. |
| **Logs** | Loki or Kibana | a query bar with a service filter and level chips, saved queries per service (the investigate actions), pinned findings |
| **Deploys** | Argo CD or GitHub deployments | services, versions, history and diffs; View recent deploys, Roll back, Restart pods |
| **DB console** | a psql or pgAdmin view | Inspect active connections, Raise max_connections, Fail over to replica |
| **Incident** | PagerDuty or incident.io | status (§3), severity, checklist (§4), timeline, the status page composer (Post status update), Page secondary |
| **Chat** | Slack | §7 |

- **Scenario actions** gain a `tool` field; this is content, not engine behaviour.
- **Service panels** in Dashboards show "Open in Logs · Deploys · DB console", pre-filtered to that service.
- **Layout:** the tools snap into a two-by-two layout from the Activities overview ("Arrange tools"). At 1366×657 every tool is usable maximised.

## 9. The rubber duck (PR A)

A rubber duck on the café table (and on the desktop) is the hint system: "Explain the problem to the duck." Each use costs 30 s of incident time (an engine action, `duck`), and it answers with the next unanswered checklist question, never the fix. Examples:
- "What changed recently?"
- "Is the loudest service the cause, or a victim?"
- "Did the errors stop because the cause is gone, or because something reset?"

## 10. New versions (PR A)

- **Polling:** the web app polls `/api/version` every 5 minutes and on focus. When the deployed version differs from the build's, a banner offers "A new version of Pit Wall On-Call is out. Reload", but only when no shift is running (idle or ended).
- **Chunk failures:** a lazy chunk that fails to load (Vite's `vite:preloadError`, or a rejected dynamic import) reloads the page once, guarded by `sessionStorage`. It never shows the crash screen. The crash screen stays for real errors and shows the error message for bug reports.

## 11. Transitions and the camera (PR A)

- **The Stage and the café survive New shift.** Only the incident session below them remounts. New shift from the results card:
  1. the card closes;
  2. the café cross-fades to the new seed's city (600 ms, or a cut under reduced motion);
  3. the camera eases into the laptop (900 ms);
  4. the desktop shows "Shift ready", with **no app auto-opened**.
- **Cold close lead-in:** "Fix confirmed" shows on the laptop for 1 s, then the camera eases out over 1.2 s (slow-in, slow-out). The results card follows 1.5 s after the caption.
- **Smoothness:**
  - camera moves animate one composited transform (`will-change: transform`, only while moving);
  - the live desktop freezes its per-tick re-render while the camera moves, and resumes when it settles;
  - an e2e check traces a zoom and requires no frame longer than 50 ms on the CI runner.

## 12. Café art (PR C)

- **Style:** lo-fi illustration. Warm lamp light with glow and falloff, gradients on every surface, soft shadows, a grain overlay, rain streaks and droplets on fogged glass, and bokeh from signs outside. **No flat colour field larger than 5% of the frame.**
- **Density:** 40 or more props per city across four depth layers:
  - **street:** vehicles, people with umbrellas, signs in the city's language;
  - **window and walls:** framed prints, a clock showing the city's time, string lights, shelves with books, jars, plants and records;
  - **counter:** espresso machine, grinder, cups, a pastry case, a tip jar, a chalkboard menu, a barista;
  - **room and table:** patrons at four or more tables, a rug, bags. On the player's table: the laptop with a visible keyboard deck, trackpad and stickers, the phone, a latte with art, a notebook, earbuds, and the rubber duck.
- **Real references per city** (recreated, never copied):
  - Melbourne: a Degraves Street laneway café with brick, fairy lights and a tram outside.
  - Tokyo: a kissaten with dark wood, siphon coffee, a vending machine and umbrellas outside.
  - Jakarta: a kopi susu gula aren bar with neon and ojek riders outside.
  - Yogyakarta: joglo wood, batik and a becak outside.
- **Phone (D1):** thin bezels and a pill island that expands into a live pager activity ("SEV2 · Checkout 5xx · 00:12"). A lock screen with the stack of notifications.
- **Easter eggs (six or more):**
  - a "works on my machine" sticker;
  - a "days since last incident" sign that resets to 0 on the page;
  - a sleeping café cat that purrs when clicked;
  - a patron whose screen says `git push --force`;
  - a "HUG OPS" poster;
  - a wall clock at 3:00 teasing "Disk Full at 3AM".
- **Motion and budget:** motion covers parallax, steam, rain, light flicker and idle loops, all still under reduced motion. The café chunk stays under 150 KB gzip.

## 13. Out of scope
- The daily (M3), more scenarios (M4, apart from training), the terminal (M6).
- Accounts, and a real usability test with people (later, per Rafif).

## 14. Review Focus (for all three PRs)
1. A player who mitigates but does not fix is told the incident is still open within 20 s, without being told the answer.
2. New shift from any state (café, laptop, mid-transition) lands on the desktop, with no app open and no jump cut.
3. A tab from before a deploy never shows the crash screen when it opens an app.
4. The async ask action replays identically on the server (same tick semantics), and old action logs still score the same.
5. At 1366×657 every tool is usable, and the results card fits without scrolling its buttons away.
